Yes — Douglas MacArthur did seriously propose using nuclear weapons during the Korean War, including against targets connected to China and North Korea. But the plan was never approved, mainly because of the risk of a wider war with the Soviet Union.

What MacArthur Proposed

After China entered the war in late 1950, MacArthur believed the war could be won quickly by escalating it. His proposals included:

Dropping 26–50 atomic bombs on strategic targets in Manchuria (northeast China).

Bombing Chinese supply routes and military concentrations.

Creating a radioactive “belt” across the Korean–Chinese border so Chinese troops could not cross into Korea. 

In later interviews, he even claimed he could end the war in about 10 days using around 30 nuclear bombs along the Manchurian border. 

Why the Plan Was Rejected

The U.S. president at the time, Harry S. Truman, rejected the idea for several reasons:

1. Fear of Soviet intervention

The Soviet Union had already tested its first atomic bomb in 1949.

Using nuclear weapons against China might trigger World War III if the USSR retaliated.

2. Escalation of the war beyond Korea

Truman wanted the war to remain a “limited war”, not a global conflict.

3. Political and moral concerns

The destruction seen at Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki made leaders cautious about using nuclear weapons again.

4. Uncertain military benefit

U.S. officials were not convinced nukes would decisively win the war. 

Result

The disagreement became one of the biggest civil-military conflicts in U.S. history.

In April 1951, Truman dismissed MacArthur from command for publicly challenging U.S. policy and pushing escalation. 

The war continued conventionally until the 1953 armistice.
